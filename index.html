Praying felt hard

When I was a baby Christian, praying for someone was hard.

Talk about Uncle Ruben, the pastor. I didn’t know what I was praying for — basically random things just to kill the time.

Where was my heart?

As I grew in my faith, I had more things to say, but my heart was not there.

Or maybe that was in my heart all along? From the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks. Apologize to the people you prayed for: “God must not have heard.” :D Then transition to what prayer is and what it isn’t.

Rejoice in hope

Let your rejoicing be rooted in hope.

Rejoice: from the Greek word chairo, to be glad, joyful, to rejoice. The present participle: being joyful or glad. We are called to an active, present state of gladness, rooted in hope. Not “rejoice while you hope,” but “let your rejoicing be rooted in hope.”

Our hope

The coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and the future redemption of our bodies.

Read Romans 8:22–25.

This hope is not yet visible, so we wait for it with patience. Verse 25 transitions to the second part of our text: be patient in tribulation.

Patient in tribulation

Remain under. Keep steady under pressure. Endure firmly.

Tribulation: pressure, crushing, affliction. Hardships, troubles, and opposition that press in on us. Paul says to endure steadfastly as the world’s pressure presses down on us.

Feel the weight

Feel the whole weight of it without abandoning your post.

This does not mean denying life’s difficulties, problems, or the weight of the world. Share about how emotions need to be felt and how the Psalmist handles them. Transition to how prayer wraps these two ideas: rejoicing in hope and remaining patient in trouble.

Constant in prayer

Remain devoted to prayer. Attend constantly to it.

As we rejoice in hope we cannot yet see, we need to pray in the present moment. As we are crushed and afflicted, pray.

In joy, pray.
In trouble, pray.

You are rejoicing, pray. You are in trouble, pray.

Prayer sustains us

Praying constantly sustains rejoicing in hope and being patient in tribulation.

I think this is why Paul puts prayer last. Hope remains alive and we can continue to stand in the fire because we have a God who is near us. We have a God who hears us and is not silent — and so we pray.
